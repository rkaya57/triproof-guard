# ScamGuard V2 — 200-case Second Holdout candidate composition

Date: 2026-08-11

## Status

This dataset is a **candidate validation set**, not an activation-grade blind field Holdout. It was assembled during post-Holdout calibration and therefore cannot independently authorize ScamGuard V2 production activation.

The production V1 decision path remains unchanged. V2 remains observe-only.

## Dataset shape

The deterministic candidate ledger contains exactly 200 cases across eight contexts: Backpack, Kamino, Orca, Marinade, Aave, 1inch, Lido, and Safe.

Surface allocation:

- URL: 48
- Token: 48
- Transaction: 64
- Wallet: 40

Ground truth allocation:

- Benign: 128
- Malicious/adversarial: 72

Every context contributes exactly 25 cases. Transaction source-context coverage is 100%. Candidate IDs, target fingerprints, and provenance IDs are unique and no target fingerprint is reused from the original 150-case seen fixture.

## Two validation strata

### Registry / first-party negatives

85 cases use registry-backed or first-party evidence. These include official project domains, public token registries, and documented protocol/account addresses where available. Their purpose is primarily false-positive and compatibility testing.

### Controlled robustness fixtures

115 cases are deterministic controlled fixtures. The malicious/adversarial subset uses RFC-reserved `.example` domains and semantic signing payloads such as ERC-20 unlimited approvals or SPL delegate approvals. These cases test whether ScamGuard understands dangerous signing semantics and suspicious origin context.

Controlled fixtures **must not be represented as observed real-world scams or as field detection accuracy**. They exist to test deterministic robustness and regression behavior. They should always be reported separately from registry/first-party cases.

## Activation boundary

The 200-case candidate validation may be used to:

- detect parser and surface regressions;
- measure controlled transaction-semantic coverage;
- measure false positives on registry/first-party negatives;
- compare V1 and additive V2 behavior without changing production decisions.

It may **not** be used by itself to activate V2. Production activation still requires a fresh, externally sourced, real-world blind set collected after the final freeze, with model-independent ground truth and sufficient malicious field cases.

## Reproducibility

The candidate ledger is generated by `scripts/generate-scamguard-v2-second-holdout-candidates.mjs`. Generation is seen-fixture-aware: targets already present in `holdout-150.csv` are excluded before selection. The generation workflow writes the deterministic ledger back to the calibration branch.

The readiness workflow validates size, class/surface coverage, provenance uniqueness, source-context coverage, dual-source metadata, and seen-target isolation. The stratified validation workflow reports overall metrics and separate controlled versus registry/first-party metrics.
